Page2.html A River Runs Through It: Story Summary

A River Runs Through It is a semi-autobiographical novella by Norman Maclean, published in 1976 and set in western Montana.

Norman and his younger brother Paul grow up in Missoula, the sons of a strict Presbyterian minister. Their father teaches them that faith and fly fishing are closely linked, and he drills them in the craft until they master it. Fishing becomes the family's way of connecting with each other and with the world around them.

As adults, the brothers take different paths. Norman is steady and reserved. Paul is charming and gifted, an artist with a fly rod, but he drinks heavily, gambles, and keeps getting into trouble. Norman tries to help his brother, but Paul resists, and the family learns that you can't always help the people you love.

The story reaches its high point on a fishing trip along the Big Blackfoot River. Paul is at his best, landing the biggest fish of the day. The moment shows the beauty and grace that coexist with his self-destruction.

Soon afterward, Paul is beaten to death in a Montana town, most likely because of his gambling debts. Norman and his father are left grieving and searching for answers. They come to accept that people can love one another completely without ever fully understanding them.

The novella ends with an elderly Norman fishing alone on the river. He reflects on his family, the passage of time, and the way the river has run through all of it.
